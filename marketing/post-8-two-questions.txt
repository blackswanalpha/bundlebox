I published the experiment that could prove my own tool worthless.

bundlebox claims the same work on less context. Those are two claims, and only
one has been run. The measured one: on SWE-bench Verified, 100 instances, the
packed brief put 2.8k tokens in the window against 8.7k, and the maintainer's
own changed files were in scope 64.3% of the time against 24.6%. Both arms
count tokens over text on disk. Neither called a model.

The unmeasured one is what anybody sensible asks next: does the smaller window
cost you any outcome? I could not answer that, so I wrote down how it would be
answered — a non-inferiority trial with the margin fixed before any data. M =
7.5 points, chosen because a tool costing you more than one task in thirteen is
not worth its install, whatever it saves on tokens.

Then the sizing, which is where it got uncomfortable. McNemar's test needs 210
to 489 tasks at that margin. DeepSWE has 113. Running both arms over all of
them is 226 trials and roughly $2,676, and it would still be underpowered. So
the honest sentence is that I cannot yet answer the question I wrote down — and
a tool that will not tell you what would falsify it is not making a claim.
